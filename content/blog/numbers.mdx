Ages, dates, phone numbers, innuendos, memes, zip codes -- there are many reasons to write a song about a specific number.
For the last year I've been collecting them in a sprawling Spotify playlist called *numbers*.
It was originally started as a challenge to see if Spotify's recommendation AI could figure out the theme of the playlist (it can't), but since then, collecting these songs has become a fun mini hobby, and *numbers* is now over 150 songs strong.

## 28

<SpotifyTrackEmbed
  id="0pYacDCZuRhcrwGUA5nTBe"
  artist={["IU", "SUGA"]}
  title="Eight"
/>

Don't tell me it's sorted incorrectly; *eight* refers to the number 28, the age of both artists when they made the song.
IU's other "age series" songs are, as discussed above, [*Twenty-three*](https://open.spotify.com/track/3YkDslZvvMixTwgDId0aYB) and [*Palette*](https://open.spotify.com/track/3y7ByLZ05tluscOTRgEJ9Y).
So why "eight" and not "twenty-eight"?
The best explanation I've seen is that "eight" (transliterated 에잇) [sounds like a Korean expression of "dammit"](https://doolsetbangtan.wordpress.com/2020/05/06/iu-eight-prod-feat-suga/).

## 2000

<SpotifyTrackEmbed
  id="4aTYZL8uyGXAdmWOQy4Wgx"
  artist="Michael Jackson"
  title="2000 Watts"
/>

> ***Two-thousand** watts, eight ohms, two-hundred volts, real strong*
>
> -- Michael Jackson

Forgive bbno\$, even the King of Pop makes mathematical errors in his music.
At $8\ \Omega$ and $200\ \mathrm{V}$, the formula $P = \frac{V^2}{R}$ (derived from [Ohm's law](https://www.electronics-tutorials.ws/dccircuits/dcp_2.html)) means that you should actually have $5000\ \mathrm{W}$ of power.

Or does it?
That calculation above assumes that "eight ohms" refers to static resistance.
If your conductor is non-ohmic, then resistance separates into two measurements.
The static resistance would be given by $R = \frac{V^2}{P}$, reflecting the total voltage and power.
Meanwhile, the differential resistance $r = \frac{dV}{dI}$ could still be $8\ \Omega$, meaning that the next volt added would cause an increase of $0.125\ \mathrm{A}$ in current instead of the $0.05\ \mathrm{A}$ you could get if the resistor were ohmic.

This example of decreasing differential resistance actually occurs in things like fluorescent lights, which get better the harder they're used.
As for what device Michael Jackson was singing about?

His penis.
